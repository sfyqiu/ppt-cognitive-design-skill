# Narrative Engine

Narrative structure is the backbone of presentation cognition.

A presentation succeeds when information unfolds in the correct psychological order.

---

# Core Philosophy

Good presentations do NOT simply display information.

They guide audience cognition progressively.

Narrative design determines:

- attention flow
- comprehension rhythm
- emotional engagement
- perceived professionalism
- memory retention

---

# Narrative Objectives

The narrative engine should:

- organize information progressively
- reduce audience confusion
- maintain attention rhythm
- reveal complexity gradually
- align storytelling with audience cognition

---

# Core Narrative Principle

Information should NOT appear randomly.

Each slide should psychologically prepare for the next slide.

---

# Recommended Narrative Sequence

## Standard Cognitive Flow

1. Context
2. Problem
3. Phenomenon
4. Cause
5. Method
6. Result
7. Meaning
8. Conclusion

---

# Teaching-oriented Narrative

For educational presentations:

1. familiar concept
2. intuitive example
3. visual explanation
4. simplified mechanism
5. structured conclusion

Avoid:

- abstract jumps
- formula-first teaching
- dense theoretical introduction

---

# Academic Narrative

For research presentations:

1. research background
2. scientific gap
3. research objective
4. methodology
5. validation
6. results
7. interpretation
8. contribution

---

# Interview Narrative

For teaching interviews:

Narrative should prioritize:

- credibility
- teaching clarity
- logic visibility
- confidence
- low defensive risk

Recommended structure:

1. establish relevance
2. demonstrate expertise
3. simplify complexity
4. show teaching capability
5. conclude with educational value

---

# Cognitive Rhythm Control

Narrative pacing should alternate between:

- dense information
- visual breathing space
- explanation
- visual reinforcement
- summary

Avoid:

- continuous dense slides
- uninterrupted technical detail
- excessive text accumulation

---

# Narrative Density Adaptation

## High Expertise Audience

Can tolerate:

- higher abstraction
- denser terminology
- compressed explanation

---

## Low Expertise Audience

Requires:

- slower pacing
- visual reinforcement
- simplified terminology
- progressive explanation

---

# Narrative Transition Logic

Every slide transition should answer:

Why should the audience continue watching?

---

# Slide-to-slide Relationship

Each slide should perform one of the following:

- introduce
- explain
- prove
- compare
- simplify
- reinforce
- summarize
- transition

---

# Narrative Failure Signals

The narrative is failing if:

- audience loses orientation
- too many concepts appear simultaneously
- no clear progression exists
- visual complexity exceeds comprehension
- transitions feel abrupt

---

# Final Principle

A presentation is not:

a document.

It is:

a guided cognitive journey.
