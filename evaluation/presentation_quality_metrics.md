# Presentation Quality Metrics System

This system defines quantitative evaluation dimensions for presentation intelligence.

Presentation quality should be measurable.

The goal is to evaluate:

- cognitive efficiency
- communication quality
- narrative clarity
- visual stability
- audience adaptation

Quantitative evaluation improves optimization capability.

---

# Core Principle

A presentation should be evaluated by:

communication effectiveness

NOT:

visual decoration complexity.

---

# Evaluation Dimensions

## 1. Cognitive Clarity Score

Measures:

- comprehension speed
- information readability
- conceptual simplicity
- hierarchy visibility

High score characteristics:

- clear focal points
- low reading burden
- rapid understanding
- stable hierarchy

Low score characteristics:

- excessive complexity
- dense information
- unstable attention flow

---

## 2. Narrative Continuity Score

Measures:

- storytelling smoothness
- transition quality
- pacing consistency
- audience orientation stability

High score characteristics:

- natural progression
- strong transitions
- stable cognitive flow

Low score characteristics:

- abrupt structure
- fragmented sections
- pacing instability

---

## 3. Visual Hierarchy Score

Measures:

- focal-point visibility
- visual guidance
- reading direction clarity
- hierarchy stability

High score characteristics:

- dominant visual anchors
- clear emphasis
- stable scanning rhythm

Low score characteristics:

- equal-weight layouts
- visual competition
- chaotic eye movement

---

## 4. Audience Adaptation Score

Measures:

- terminology suitability
- pacing suitability
- abstraction control
- communication accessibility

High score characteristics:

- audience-aligned complexity
- effective explanation depth
- adaptive pacing

Low score characteristics:

- audience mismatch
- over-technical explanation
- cognitive overload

---

## 5. Knowledge Compression Score

Measures:

- simplification efficiency
- abstraction reduction
- meaning density
- explanation efficiency

High score characteristics:

- concise communication
- intuitive understanding
- efficient diagrams
- semantic clarity

Low score characteristics:

- raw complexity exposure
- excessive detail
- weak compression

---

## 6. Visual Rhythm Score

Measures:

- slide pacing variation
- whitespace balance
- visual breathing space
- presentation fatigue control

High score characteristics:

- alternating slide rhythm
- stable visual pacing
- audience comfort

Low score characteristics:

- repetitive structures
- continuous dense slides
- visual exhaustion

---

## 7. Risk-control Score

Measures:

- questioning resilience
- clarity of positioning
- claim stability
- cognitive defensibility

High score characteristics:

- restrained positioning
- stable claims
- controlled complexity
- transparent logic

Low score characteristics:

- over-claiming expertise
- unstable hierarchy
- excessive exposure

---

# Composite Evaluation Logic

The system should combine:

- cognitive metrics
- narrative metrics
- visual metrics
- audience metrics
- risk metrics

to estimate overall presentation intelligence quality.

---

# Optimization Logic

If scores decline:

the system should:

- simplify structure
- reduce density
- improve hierarchy
- increase readability
- strengthen narrative continuity

Evaluation should directly guide optimization.

---

# Final Principle

Presentation quality succeeds when:

understanding becomes efficient,
stable,
and cognitively comfortable.
